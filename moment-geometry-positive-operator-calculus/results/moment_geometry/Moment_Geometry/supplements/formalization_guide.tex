\section{Purpose, evidence, and source conventions}

This guide specifies reusable interfaces for the results in
\emph{Moment geometry and positive operator calculus}. It is intended for
independent verification and subsequent formalization. The paper contains
the mathematical proofs. The accompanying \path{theorem_registry.json}
records individual statements, assumptions, dependencies, external inputs,
and relevant executable checks. Literal identifiers such as
\texttt{geo:sharp} are the source labels in the paper; they are not theorem
numbers in this document. Paths are relative to the distribution root.

There are four distinct kinds of evidence. A \emph{mathematical proof}
is the argument in the paper, with the cited external theorems. An
\emph{exact finite check} evaluates an integer, rational, cyclotomic, or
symbolic identity for specified inputs. A \emph{certified arithmetic
check} supplies exact inequalities or outward enclosures for a specified
finite certificate. A \emph{numerical diagnostic} checks finite matrices
or samples with stated tolerances. The last three categories support
reproduction and can expose errors, but do not replace an all-degree or
analytic proof. No complete proof-assistant verification is claimed in
this version. In particular, a successful symbolic calculation is not a
formal verification of the symbolic algebra system.

The registry therefore uses \texttt{proof\_supplied} for the mathematical
status and separately sets \texttt{proof\_assistant\_verified} to false.
These are independent of whether a finite script passed. Open questions
are stored in a separate list and are never promoted to proved records.

\section{Geometric and analytic theorem contracts}

\paragraph{Positive moment repair and energy.}
The finite-dimensional starting point is \texttt{prop:repair}, with
the explicit coherent dual frame \texttt{eq:repairframe} and covariance
spectrum \texttt{an:frame-spectrum}. A qutrit probability measure with
second-moment error $\delta$ is mixed, with retained weight
$(1+102\delta)^{-1}$, with a measure of density in $[1/2,3/2]$ to obtain
an exact second design. The entropy estimate is a direct consequence of
convexity. The exact verifier is
\path{supplements/ensembles/verify_analytic_constants.py}.
Its finite frame identities precede the separate pluripotential theorem
\texttt{an:dilution}, which bounds the energy retained by that positive
mixing. Formalization should keep the moment map, the positivity of the
repair density, and the extended-valued energy inequality as separate
interfaces. The frame calculation alone does not prove continuity of
energy under arbitrary weak convergence.

\paragraph{Sharp first-moment threshold.}
\texttt{geo:sharp} asserts the sharp coefficient
$d/(d+1-H_{d+1})$ for balanced measures on $\mathbb P^d$.
Every strictly subcritical entropy inequality holds, and every strictly
supercritical one fails. It does not claim an endpoint inequality.
The proof has six interfaces: balanced allocation of monomials;
the zero moment map for a determinant-twisted minimal-jet tensor;
a semi-invariant from geometric invariant theory; a valuation estimate
for the balanced basis ideal; exact fixed-degree Hilbert comparison;
and a well-conditioned coupling followed by removal of a Haar floor.
The sharpness construction is a separate family of balanced coordinate
caps. This separation avoids identifying a basis-ideal calculation with
an integrability statement for the full many-particle determinant.

The jet chain rule must retain all lower-order terms until minimality
annihilates them; its cost is measured together with the Jacobian
discrepancy on a resolving chart. The finite checks are
\path{supplements/projective_jets/verify_jet_moment_map.py},
\path{supplements/projective_jets/verify_projective_jet_extension.py}, and
\path{supplements/geometry/verify_truncated_jet_chain.py}.
They check specified weights and symbolic chains, not resolution of
singularities or all valuations.

For \texttt{geo:exact-quantization}, the precise external input is the
Hilbert-geodesic comparison in \citet[Proposition 4.1]{geo_zhang2024}.
The absence of an error at fixed degree is a specialization proved in
the paper: along the weak geodesic, the auxiliary curvature is
$M(\omega_{\rm FS}+dd^c\psi_t)+(d+1)\omega_{\rm FS}\ge0$;
the initial Bergman density is constant; and the affine Hilbert energy
is compared with its initial derivative. One must not import the
general asymptotic comparison in that reference as an epsilon-free
fixed-degree assertion. The identical specialization underlies
\texttt{an:lower-comparison}.

\paragraph{Constrained entropy and equilibrium.}
\texttt{an:entropy-projection} assumes a finite-energy potential,
a bounded external field, and the finite second-design moment map.
Exponential integrability, coercivity of the finite-dimensional dual,
and strict convexity give a unique multiplier and a unique entropy
projection for the fixed potential. The Pythagorean identity and the
explicit multiplier bound are useful independent formalization targets.
The external finite-energy integrability input is
\citet[Proposition 1.4]{BBEGZ2019}.

\texttt{an:equilibrium} combines this projection with
\texttt{geo:sharp}, the variational pluripotential theory of
\citet{BBGZ2013}, and elliptic regularity. For smooth $W$ and
$0\le\gamma<12/7$, the second-design free energy
$H-\gamma E+\int W$ attains its minimum; every minimizer has a smooth
strictly positive density solving the displayed constrained
Monge--Amp\`ere equation. Uniform estimates apply on compact parameter
ranges with controlled smooth fields. Uniqueness of the minimizing
measure is not part of this theorem. The threshold $12/7$ is sufficient
here because second designs are balanced; it is not asserted to be the
sharp second-design threshold.

\texttt{an:lower-comparison} constructs continuous finite-test envelopes
$E_r$ with
$E_k\ge E_r(L_N)-(2\log k+C_r)/k$ on the projective plane.
The proof combines the exact Hilbert comparison, a local Bergman bound,
and Hadamard's determinant inequality. The bound includes determinant
zeros by allowing $E_k=+\infty$. Its direction is essential in the
repulsive large-deviation proof below.

\paragraph{Algebraic supports, entanglement, and surfaces.}
\texttt{geo:cubic} classifies ternary cubic zero sets carrying a
projective second design up to \emph{unitary} change of coordinates.
A linear determinantal representation produces a three-qutrit tensor.
Two-factor balancing, a rank-two matrix inequality, and the second-design
identity force balance on the third factor. The closed-orbit Cartan
classification for the relevant $E_6$ grading is then an essential
external theorem, not a numerical orbit test
\citep{geo_sauerwein2018,geo_ditrani2023}. The smooth case has the unique
uniform Hesse measure; the triangle case retains the stated phase-measure
freedom. Determinant and tensor identities are checked by
\path{supplements/ensembles/verify_cubic_classification.py}.

The independent support certificate \texttt{geo:hesse-bound} needs no
prior finite-support assumption: a positive three-copy witness controls
distance to the Hesse support, while the one-copy SIC Gram matrix
controls its weights. The general form \texttt{geo:general-cert}
isolates exactly these two hypotheses. The confidence statement
\texttt{geo:confidence} additionally requires the access model in the
dictionary and fixed-size independent trials. Exact identities are in
\path{supplements/ensembles/verify_hesse_rigidity.py} and
\path{supplements/ensembles/verify_hesse_ensemble_certification.py}.
The statistical implementation evaluates binomial quantiles numerically;
it does not supply interval-certified quantiles.

\texttt{geo:singer-nef} has a different purpose: the known ten-point
design of \citet{geo_iosue2024} does not admit the stated nef nine-point
coupling, and its Haar dilution admits one exactly for $t\le20/21$.
The proof uses an occupancy inequality and an explicit extremal coupling.
\texttt{geo:k3-lattice} constructs the associated K3 surface and
identifies its transcendental lattice as $A_2(7)$. Polynomial and
intersection computations are finite, whereas the final lattice
identification uses the classification of the specified symplectic and
non-symplectic actions
\citep{geo_garbagnati_sarti2007,geo_artebani_sarti_taki2011}.
The supporting program is
\path{supplements/geometry/verify_singer_nef_obstruction.py}.

\paragraph{Operational contact geometry.}
\texttt{geo:operational} concerns fixed finite outcome number. At
$\eta_d=(H_d-1)/(d-1)$ it gives a positive neighborhood of the
projective-simulable set; for qutrits at $5/12$ it gives the larger
neighborhood of the convex hull of reducible POVMs. The radius is
qualitative and may depend on the outcome number. The anisotropic
flagged-contact inequality \texttt{geo:flag-theorem}, the negative-cusp
lemma \texttt{geo:cusp}, and deletion of zero labels supply the compactness
argument. \texttt{geo:boundary} classifies contacts inside these center
classes. The exact checks in \path{supplements/qutrit/} validate angular
identities and rational coefficient margins; they do not compute a
universal neighborhood radius or establish simulation of every
irreducible qutrit POVM.

\section{Probability and combinatorial theorem contracts}

\paragraph{Coherent spectral law.}
\texttt{lem:coherent-graph-integral} localizes the graph integral at its
maximal-modulus diagonal. The pinned directed Laplacian is accretive,
and its determinant is the rooted directed spanning-tree count.
This analytic lemma leads to \texttt{thm:coherent-spectral-law}:
\[
 M_p(q,\rho)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathfrak P(p)}
       \rho^{|\pi|-1}\tau(G_\pi)^{-q}.
\]
Parallel directed edges must be retained; loops do not contribute to
the Laplacian. The convention for the single-vertex tree count is one.
The proof establishes convergence of expected moments, variance
$O(n_m^{-1})$, moment determinacy, and convergence in probability.
Almost-sure convergence is asserted when $\sum_m n_m^{-1}<\infty$.
Conditioning on a finite family of bounded moments preserves the law
when the tube width obeys $n_m\epsilon_m^2\to\infty$.

\texttt{lem:tree-one-noncrossing} identifies $\tau=1$ precisely with
noncrossing partitions. \texttt{thm:coherent-free-limit} then proves
unbounded support at every fixed finite $q$, an entire moment-generating
function with a Poisson-type upper bound, and convergence to the
mean-one Marchenko--Pastur law as $q\to\infty$. The aspect-ratio weight
is $\rho^{|\pi|-1}$; Kreweras complementation reconciles it with the
alternative standard convention. At $q=2,\rho=1$, the fourth moment is
$57/4$, not $14$. Directed-tree methods connect the proof to spatial
random-matrix moment expansions \citep{prob_bordenave2008}; the
noncrossing layer uses standard free-probability combinatorics
\citep{prob_nica_speicher2006}.

\path{supplements/probability/verify_coherent_gram_moments.py} checks
finite graph expansions. The independent
\path{supplements/probability/verify_probability_extensions.py} checks
5,295 partitions through order eight, Narayana counts, 124 pairings,
and rational aspect-ratio formulas. The arbitrary-order analytic and
combinatorial proofs remain necessary. No positive-moment assertion
controls eigenvalues on exponentially small scales.

\paragraph{Microscopic repair and repulsive pressure.}
\texttt{lem:conditional-replacement} is a one-coordinate inverse-moment
estimate with exponent $s<1/3$ on the projective plane. Along projective
lines, the polynomial factors and H\"older's inequality gives the
constant $2/[(1-3s)(2-3s)]$. Its iteration controls the energy cost of
replacing $m$ coordinates. In
\texttt{prop:microscopic-moment-repair}, positive finite-dimensional
moment repair and two conditioning steps correct a fixed design order
at entropy cost at most $m\log(3/2)+2\log2$, with $m=o(N)$.
The exact constants are checked in
\path{supplements/probability/verify_microscopic_repair.py}.

\texttt{thm:repulsive-design-ldp} assumes fixed design order $t$,
tube width $N^{-a}$ with $0<a<1/2$, and fixed $\beta\ge0$.
It proves pressure convergence for every continuous empirical-law tilt
and the full LDP with rate $H+\beta E$ restricted to the design fibre.
Its lower bound imports the unrestricted positive-temperature LDP in
\citet{prob_berman2017} and uses microscopic repair. Its upper bound
uses constrained Sanov and \texttt{an:lower-comparison}. This import is
for a positively polarized manifold and repulsive sign; it is not an
attractive LDP theorem. The conditioned reference has probability
tending to one, which also justifies its use in the coherent spectral
law.

\paragraph{Attractive pressure from compact conditionals.}
The full proof is in \path{proofs/attractive_high_temperature.tex}.
Its reusable core is \texttt{att:quadratic-pressure}: for a reference
$\nu=g\sigma$ with a fixed $L^p$ bound, $p>1$, the centered pressure
satisfies $0\le p_k(t)-t\mathbb E_{\nu^N}E_k\le Ct^2$ for
\emph{both signs} of small $t$, uniformly in $k$. Full-conditional
entropy tensorization is essential. Importing only the repulsive
pressure then proves \texttt{att:general-product-energy}, the actual
product-law mean and $L^1$ energy limit. This does not assume an
attractive limit at the input stage.

For $\gamma$ below the normalized-psh exponential threshold,
\texttt{att:directing-class} puts attractive Gibbs conditionals in a
compact $L^r$ family with a positive lower bound. Lipschitz empirical
tilts have compact one-coordinate increments; arbitrary continuous
tilts are handled only after uniform approximation. The exact spatial
coarse-graining inequality \texttt{att:coarse-entropy} and finite type
counts yield \texttt{att:entropy-chaos}. Finite smooth reference nets
have uniformly controlled exponential constants. The summed conditional
entropy identity then transfers product mean energy to the actual
Gibbs laws in \texttt{att:gibbs-energy-limit}. Conditioning on the
reference cells need not preserve conditional regularity; only the
original Gibbs law uses that hypothesis.

\texttt{att:good-rate} writes $H-\gamma E$ as an infimum over the
compact normalized-psh family of relative entropy against
$e^{-\gamma u}\sigma$ minus potential energy. This reference is
unnormalized. Joint lower semicontinuity and the strict exponential
margin justify the infimum and treat infinite entropy without subtracting
infinities. The same lemma gives energy recovery whenever entropy
converges. These inputs prove \texttt{att:full-laplace} and
\texttt{att:design-laplace}, for every continuous empirical-law tilt.
\texttt{att:polarized-extension} covers every positive polarized compact
complex manifold for $\gamma<\alpha(\omega,\sigma)$; fixed-order design
tubes are asserted on projective spaces, where unitary heat averaging
preserves the constraint. This proof is analytic; no finite matrix test
is offered as a replacement for collective energy convergence.

\paragraph{Canonical curvature and exact response.}
\path{proofs/attractive_concentration.tex} applies H\"ormander's
minimal-solution estimate to top-degree forms valued in the
anticanonical bundle \citep{Hormander1965}. The regularized determinant
metric retains curvature at least $(1-\gamma)G_M$.
\texttt{ac:variance}, \texttt{ac:concentration}, and \texttt{ac:tubes}
hold whenever $\gamma<1$ and the finite-level partition exists;
\texttt{ac:integrability} supplies the unconditional range
$\gamma<1/(q+1)$. The exact Ward identity \texttt{ac:ward}
comes from infinitesimal projective transformations and fixes the
first-moment covariance. These assertions must distinguish integrability
from curvature. Combined with the full LDP, exponential separation
proves \texttt{att:haar-minimizer}: pressure zero and the unique Haar
minimizer in the proved projective range. Mere empirical convergence
would not establish uniqueness of the rate-zero set.

\paragraph{Toric assignment certificates.}
\path{proofs/toric_assignment.tex} gives an independent constructive
bridge. \texttt{tor:vertices} exposes every assignment monomial by a
sum-of-squares separation and uses one-circle Jensen to bound the
phase-averaged log determinant. Exact multinomial modal probabilities
give \texttt{tor:finite-certificate}, with error at most
$2\log N/k$ in the energy. \texttt{tor:cost-limit} permits logarithmic
boundary singularities under the explicit integrability assumption
$\int\sum_j-\log p_j\,d\eta<\infty$; its truncated-cost proof controls
all couplings by one marginal tail. Toric Legendre duality and
Kantorovich duality identify the limit in \texttt{tor:energy-transport}
\citep{BermanWittNystrom2014,BBGZ2013}. Neither these phase-average
bounds nor positive spectral moments imply angular inverse-moment
bounds. The general product-energy theorem and this assignment formula
have different purposes: one supplies the LDP input, the other an
explicit optimization problem.

\paragraph{The nonlinear Green formula and interpolation sampling.}
\path{proofs/nonlinear_green.tex} addresses the determinant instance of
\citet[Problem 4.10 and Conjecture 6.1]{prob_berman2017}.
In \texttt{cy:finite-convexity}, retain $N-2$ evaluation rows and reduce
the remaining determinant to a two-section pencil. The exact Hessian
\texttt{cy:hessian-hierarchy} is $(N-1)/k$ times an average of
$\sum_{j\ge1}j^{-1}\|\int P^{\otimes j}\,d\eta\|_{\rm HS}^2$.
Signed logarithmic integrability justifies the positive series; fixed
bases must be used when varying the reference volume. Convex derivative
convergence and normalized-psh compactness prove
\texttt{cy:green-formula}. Yau's theorem identifies the limit
\citep{Yau1978}; uniform exponential tails give every finite $L^p$
topology. The equation is $(\omega+dd^c\phi)^q/V=\mu$, with
$V=\int\omega^q$ and $\int\phi\,d\mu=0$.

\texttt{cy:interpolation-sampling} averages the zero divisor of the
section through $N-1$ independent points. Its currents converge weakly.
Mean-zero section potentials have squared $L^2$ sampling error at most
$2C_a(\mu)/(a^2M)$; $J$ independent calibration points per section
replace exact centering with the factor $1+1/J$. Evaluation of sections,
the Hermitian norm, and independent sample access to $\mu$ are the
specified inputs. The polynomial-level bias has no effective rate, and
no convergence of finite Hessians or of metric derivatives is claimed.
The zero-free hypothesis used for the conditional predecessor in
\citet[Theorems 3.2--3.3]{Berman2023zeta} is not a theorem input here.

\paragraph{Ordered logarithms.}
\texttt{op:heap} expresses the logarithm coefficient of a heap through
the derivative of its order polynomial, or equivalently a signed
descent sum over linear extensions. The dependence relation is symmetric
and reflexive, and the identity is a formal graded identity. Standard
heap theory and order-polynomial theory supply its algebraic foundations
\citep{CartierFoata1969,Viennot1986,Stanley1986}.
The finite example \texttt{op:root-response} demonstrates that a scalar
polymer bound does not by itself control the absolute ordered Taylor
series. This is a limitation on that inference, not a failure of the
positive resummation used next.

\section{Positive operator calculus and gap contracts}

\paragraph{Clocks, metrics, and finite certificates.}
For Hermitian $G$, \texttt{op:hyperbolic-clock} identifies
$(\operatorname{ad}_G/\sinh\operatorname{ad}_G)^\tau$ with a positive
Gamma-subordinated heat channel for every $\tau\ge0$.
All ordinary operator-norm error estimates remain valid with an
arbitrary ancillary identity channel. The mean-corrected truncation
uses a fourth nested commutator, not a global bandwidth estimate.
The probability input is the infinitely divisible hyperbolic product
\citep{PitmanYor2003}.

\texttt{op:metric-range} identifies exactly the Bessel parameters
$\nu\ge-1/2$ for which the associated mean is operator monotone.
The proof uses a positive beta mixture of powers; the converse violates
operator concavity. Petz's correspondence supplies the metric
interpretation \citep{Petz1996,HiaiKosakiPetzRuskai2013}.
For \texttt{op:quadrature-theorem}, the input measure must be positive
with bounded support and the required nondegenerate moment matrices.
Gaussian and Radau interpolation yield positive finite clocks and
observable-specific commutator errors. Finite-support degeneracies
terminate exactly. The derived \texttt{op:information-bracket} is a
fixed-state information-matrix bound; it does not declare every
approximating rule a globally monotone metric.

Reusable checks are the following programs.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \path{supplements/modular/positive_bessel_filters.py}
\item \path{supplements/modular/finite_arithmetic_certificate.py}
\item \path{supplements/modular/local_commutator_certificate.py}
\item \path{supplements/modular/score_and_metric_audit.py}
\end{itemize}
The first supplies exact recurrence checks and separate diagnostics;
the second isolates algebraic nodes by Sturm intervals and budgets
rounding errors; the third combines Pauli coefficients before bounding
commutator norms. \path{supplements/modular/lee_yang_compiler.py}
exhibits the positive-zero-measure interface beyond the Bessel family.

\paragraph{Compatible response and its boundaries.}
\texttt{q:thm-powers} proves the exact response of unnormalized
fractional powers along faithful differentiable matrix paths.
The Hermitian and anti-Hermitian filters are completely positive with
norms $r$ and $r^2$, respectively, for $0\le r\le1$.
Their arguments are compatible through
$Q^*=e^{-\operatorname{ad}_K}Q$; they cannot be prescribed independently.
\texttt{q:prop-rotation} represents the logarithmic response as
$\dot K=H+i[C,K]$ with $\|C\|\le\|J\|/3$.
The finite response and obstruction checks are in
\path{supplements/modular_response/verify_power_response_and_obstructions.py}.

The explicit examples \texttt{q:prop-ratio-obstruction} and
\texttt{q:prop-filter-obstruction} are distinct results.
Local conditional ratios do not force exact locality of a logarithmic
difference, and an operator-norm CP contraction does not force a bound
in the same weighted interaction norm. Neither example rules out
spatial decay under additional dynamical or geometric hypotheses.

\paragraph{Network gaps and microscopic transfer.}
\texttt{q:thm-wire-gap} assumes finite simple graphs, nonzero cores,
full-Schmidt-rank bonds, and explicit CP sandwiches of each wire by a
faithful rank-one map. The two-port estimate \texttt{q:lem-schur}
converts these sandwiches into subspace angles, independently of core
condition number. A nonnegative conflict matrix $C$ with $\rho(C)<1$
gives blocked gap at least $1-\rho(C)$.
\texttt{q:lem-seed} transfers this result only when each selected
microscopic patch has the stated ground image and finite gap $g_*>0$.

\texttt{q:cor-aklt} verifies these hypotheses for the specified
decorated AKLT networks of maximum degree $\Delta$ and even decoration
$n=2\ell$. The transfer radius is
$2/[3^\ell+(-1)^\ell]$. The sufficient logarithmic decoration estimate
is uniform in graph size, while its physical gap retains the finite-patch
factor. This does not assert a gap for every undecorated AKLT lattice.
The core-independent channel estimate and the polynomial
ground-space argument should be formalized separately.
The reusable certificate is
\path{supplements/networks/channel_gap_compiler.py}, with exact tests
in \path{supplements/networks/test_channel_gap_compiler.py}.
\path{supplements/networks/verify_two_port_schur_angle.py} is a bounded
floating diagnostic, not the proof of the quotient-metric lemma.

\texttt{q:thm-oblique} is an independent certificate for commuting
idempotents, expressed through their adjoint commutators and
orthogonalization. \texttt{q:cor-filtered} specializes it to bounded,
local, invertible product filters of commuting projectors. Here locality
comes from the supplied product structure; it is not inferred from a
generic modular conjugation.

\section{Reproduction and a formalization order}

From \path{supplements/}, run \path{verify_all.py} with the supplied
dependency versions. The optional \texttt{-{}-include-diagnostics} switch
adds bounded floating checks; Monte Carlo requires a separate explicit
option. The aggregate record \path{supplements/results/verification_report.json}
contains commands, arithmetic classes, versions, hashes, and output paths.
Individual scripts and their finite domains are listed in the README.
Assertions must remain enabled. An omitted optional arithmetic backend
is recorded as an omission, rather than as a successful certificate.

A useful formalization order begins with finite interfaces: coherent
moments, rational polynomial identities, graph cofactors, Choi positivity,
and exact quadrature transcripts. It then treats the finite-dimensional
general lemmas---entropy projection, operator divided differences,
completely positive functional calculus, subspace angles, and coupling
inequalities. The next layer formalizes the all-degree combinatorics,
the local graph asymptotic, and variational convergence. Resolution,
K\"ahler-geodesic comparison, complex Monge--Amp\`ere regularity, and
the quoted orbit and lattice classifications should remain explicit
external theorem interfaces until their libraries are available.
This order allows finite certificates to be independently checked
without pretending that they settle the analytic dependencies.

Two future targets remain separate from this ledger. Extending the
attractive determinant LDP to a neighborhood of $\gamma=1$ requires
collective control beyond the scalar section-integrability mechanism.
The exact sharp threshold on the second-design fibre requires further
geometric input. The proved attractive interval, the first-moment
coefficient, and the positive Gram moment law do not settle either target.
